What is the difference between these models?
ModelCredit goes toWhy use itWhere it misleads
Last touch The final touch before converting The industry default. Simple, unambiguous, easy to reconcile with other tools. Over-credits whatever sits closest to existing intent — branded search, retargeting.
First touch The opening touch Answers what introduced this customer to you. Over-credits awareness channels and ignores everything that closed the sale.
Linear Split evenly across the path Makes no claim about which position mattered, which is honest. An incidental display impression counts as much as the email that persuaded them.
Position 40/20/40 40% first, 40% last, 20% spread across the middle Encodes an explicit belief: introduction and closing matter most. That belief may be wrong for your business, and the middle is where persuasion often happens.
Time decay Weighted exponentially toward the conversion Recency as a proxy for influence. Useful for short consideration cycles. Decays by path position rather than real elapsed time, so uneven gaps are mistreated.
Markov removal Proportional to conversion probability lost if the channel did not exist Counterfactual. Asks the question a budget decision actually needs. A channel present in most journeys looks indispensable regardless of influence.
Shapley value Average marginal contribution across every coalition of channels Counterfactual. Fair-share properties: symmetry, null player, efficiency. Expensive above ~12 channels, so it switches to sampling and rescales to tie out.

Why these seven. The first five are the standard industry set — every attribution tool implements them, so running them here lets you reconcile against whatever you already use. The last two are counterfactual models: instead of dividing credit by position, they estimate what would happen without a channel. They are well documented in the literature and rare in open source, which is most of the reason this project exists.

Run all seven and watch the spread. A channel every model treats the same way is a safe budget decision. A channel where they disagree by fifteen points is a decision resting on a modelling assumption nobody chose deliberately. And when every model agrees but all of them are wrong — see the last-touch trap — only an experiment resolves it.

How to read this chart {% if result.ground_truth %} {% endif %}
One group per channel Channels are ordered by size, largest first. Within a group there is one bar per model you selected.
Bar height That model's share of total revenue for that channel. Every model divides the same revenue, so each model's bars sum to 100% across all channels.
The grey bar True influence — the right answer. It exists only because this dataset was simulated from known channel weights. Your own data has no grey bar, which is exactly why trying a sample first is worth the two minutes.
Grey vs coloured Accuracy. A coloured bar far from the grey one means that model is wrong about that channel, and the direction tells you how.
Spread among the coloured bars Consensus. Bars at similar heights mean the models agree. A wide spread means your budget decision rests on which model you happened to pick.
The trap Agreement is not correctness. Every model can cluster tightly and all be wrong together — see The last-touch trap. When that happens, only a holdout experiment resolves it.
